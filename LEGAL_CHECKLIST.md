# Before you charge anyone: checklist

This is a list of questions to take to a lawyer and your providers, not legal advice.

## Data licensing
- [ ] **The Odds API.** Standard plans are for your own use. Showing their prices to paying users
      usually needs a commercial or redistribution agreement. Email them before launch.
- [ ] **ESPN.** The site API is unofficial and has no published terms for commercial use. For a paid
      product, plan to switch to a licensed stats feed (Sportradar, Stats Perform, SportsDataIO).
- [ ] **MLB Stats API.** Its terms limit commercial use; check them.
- [ ] Pick'em app names and logos are trademarks. Describe them plainly and don't use their logos.

## Gambling law
- [ ] Selling picks or betting tools is regulated differently by state. Some states require tout or
      affiliate licenses; some ban paid picks. Get a state-by-state answer for where you'll sell.
- [ ] Pick'em apps are legal in some states and banned or restricted in others, and that list changes.
      Consider limiting sign-ups by location.
- [ ] Advertising: no promises of profit, no targeting of under-21s, responsible-gambling messaging
      (the app shows 1-800-GAMBLER). Several states have specific ad rules.
- [ ] Age verification: the sign-up form asks users to confirm 21+. A paid product may need more.

## Business basics
- [ ] Terms of service and a privacy policy (the app has a basic terms page; have a lawyer write the
      real ones).
- [ ] Payment processors often restrict gambling-related businesses. Confirm before building billing.
- [ ] Accounts at sportsbooks and pick'em apps get limited when they win. Tell customers plainly.
- [ ] Keep the track record honest: the hash chain helps, and so does never deleting losing picks.
